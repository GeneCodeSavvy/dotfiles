# Conversion Patterns

Use this reference when Build Mode or Audit Mode needs concrete landing-page patterns and example-backed decisions. Do not treat any conversion rate as a benchmark without traffic source, sample size, conversion definition, and offer context.

Sources distilled:

- Unbounce, "15 high-converting landing page examples (+ why they work)", Garrett Hughes, April 6, 2025.
- Reddit r/PPC thread, "What is the highest converting landing page you have ever created?", extracted January-April 2026 comments.
- Local CRO video transcript about seven landing-page lessons from reviewing 1,500+ pages.

## Core Pattern

High-converting pages usually combine:

1. Message match: ad/search/email promise, hero headline, visual, CTA, and post-click expectation all point to the same offer.
2. One dominant action: the CTA is visible early, visually obvious, specific, and not surrounded by competing exits.
3. Above-the-fold proof: testimonials, reviews, media quotes, customer logos, ratings, usage metrics, screenshots, or real examples appear early enough to influence the first decision.
4. Contextual visual: show the product, service, outcome, preview, demo, or use context; avoid decorative imagery when the visitor needs confidence.
5. Scan-first structure: section headings carry the argument, body copy supports it, and every section moves the visitor closer to action.
6. Risk reduction: nearby text answers fear, uncertainty, doubt, privacy, return, trial, warranty, timing, and "what happens next" concerns.
7. Mobile and speed discipline: keep the CTA reachable, proof visible, media lightweight, forms usable, and initial load fast.

## Above-the-Fold Formula

Use this sequence unless the offer calls for an unusually minimal lead-capture page:

1. Headline: name the visitor's desired outcome or solved pain, not just the company category.
2. Subhead: explain what the product/service does and why this mechanism is different or credible.
3. Proof: include at least one specific trust signal above the fold; use two when traffic is cold or the claim is high stakes.
4. CTA: use action copy that describes the next step, such as "Get my quote", "Check eligibility", "Book a demo", or "Download the guide".
5. Risk reducer: place a short reassurance near the CTA, such as no credit card, free consultation, privacy note, warranty, response time, or cancellation policy.
6. Visual: use a screenshot, preview video, product-in-use photo, service outcome image, map/location cue, or offer mockup that reinforces the claim.

If the page is failing, test the hero before rewriting the whole page. The highest-impact tests are headline angle, CTA wording, proof placement, visual choice, form length, and risk reducer.

## Example Pattern Bank

- Promo, social media, 46.94%: used video across the first fold, explainer content, sample outputs, testimonials, and client logos. Use this pattern when the product's value is best understood through motion or before/after demonstration.
- edX, education, 52.68%: simplified the course pitch into clear outcomes and a few learning bullets. Use this pattern when the visitor already knows the category and needs clarity, not a long persuasion essay.
- Simply Business, insurance, 62.26%: made a complex product feel simple before asking visitors to complete a more involved flow. Use this pattern for regulated, financial, insurance, legal, or high-friction offers.
- Later, social media, 57.92%: kept the ad, email, creative, landing page, and follow-up offer consistent. Use this pattern for paid traffic and lead magnets where "conversion scent" matters.
- The Listings Lab, real estate: used a focused offer, guide mockup, clear pain-point copy, directional cue, and privacy reassurance. Use this pattern for local-service or professional-service lead magnets.
- Twillory, clothing, 46.85%: built a lighter mobile experience while preserving the persuasive visuals. Use this pattern when mobile traffic is high and rich desktop media would slow down first load.
- TyresOnTheDrive, automotive: led with a clear differentiator, quick process, testimonials, and brand trust. Use this pattern for local intent where convenience and trust decide the conversion.
- ooba, finance, 35.57%: made the form action clear, placed the form above the fold, and used descriptive button copy. Use this pattern when the visitor must submit personal or financial information.
- ClaimCompass, legal, 30.02%: supported a surprising offer with authority, process explanation, repeated CTAs, and education lower on the page. Use this pattern when some visitors are ready now and others need proof before acting.
- Extreme Lounging, furniture giveaway: used a minimal hero, image, headline, and email form. Use this pattern only when the incentive is obvious and the conversion is low friction.
- onX, navigation, 61.15%: matched PPC search intent with exact copy and visual cues. Use this pattern for feature-specific search campaigns.
- Investing Shortcuts, finance, 51.32%: connected the offer to urgency and cultural timing, backed by social proof. Use urgency only when it is real and tied to the offer.
- MyTutor, education, 55.29%: timed a supportive offer around exam results day. Use this pattern when the audience is in a specific life moment and needs empathy plus a clear next step.
- College Board, education, 77.38%: made the deadline visible near the CTA. Use countdowns or expiry dates only when they are factual and important to the action.
- FilterEasy, home repair, 34.52%: combined straightforward structure, benefits, social proof, and ongoing testing. Use this as the reminder that the best explanation sometimes comes from validated tests, not opinion.

## PPC and Local-Service Notes

- Put the offer and CTA completely above the fold for high-intent search traffic.
- Match the landing-page headline to the ad/search promise before optimizing lower-page sections.
- For local services, show service area, locality, trust markers, reviews, and direct contact action early.
- For lead gen, qualify the conversion definition: form fill, tracked phone call, booked appointment, trial signup, or purchase.
- Avoid bragging about raw conversion rate without context. Improvement against the same business, traffic, and conversion event is more useful than isolated numbers.
- Treat page load under roughly two seconds as a practical PPC target; slow paid pages burn budget before copy can help.

## Proof Quality Rules

Prefer proof that a visitor can understand and trust quickly:

- named customer quote with role/company or location
- photo or source when appropriate
- review source, rating count, or platform
- screenshot or product output
- publication quote, not just a logo strip
- metric with context and time range
- recognizable customer logo only when the relationship is real and relevant

Flag weak proof when it is anonymous, duplicated, unverifiable, hidden in a carousel, disconnected from the offer, or repeated without context.

## A/B Test Backlog Ideas

Choose tests that map to the page's likely blocker:

- Hero headline: outcome, pain relief, mechanism, urgency, or audience-specific angle.
- Subhead: short mechanism explanation versus proof-oriented explanation.
- CTA copy: generic action versus next-step-specific action.
- Proof placement: above fold versus after product explanation.
- Visual: product screenshot, customer outcome, preview video, offer mockup, or service-in-context photo.
- Form friction: one-step versus multi-step, shorter field set, field order, or privacy reassurance.
- Pricing or offer framing: price visible early, guarantee near CTA, deadline, bundle, discount clarity, or no-discount value framing.
- Mobile layout: sticky CTA, exposed reviews, compressed media, or reordered proof.
